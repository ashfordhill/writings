# The Landlord Spa

On a recent weekend, my husband Yuchi surprised me with a much-needed spa visit in Oak Park. Mani pedi and a 90 minute massage for just $109. I'd never been there before but his coworkers at the Oak Park Village recommended the place. I've discovered in my adult life that I enjoy getting manicures and pedicures; just like a fresh haircut, it makes me feel cleaned up.

The woman giving me the spa treatment is named Amy. Amy is a Chinese woman in her 50's with a thick accent. Yuchi spoke to her in Mandarin when he dropped me off, though her native tongue is in Cantonese since she's from the southern part of China. She whisked me off to the massage room. As the massage began, she started yappin' away. 

"Oh great", I thought. "This is going to be 90 minutes of chit-chat". 

I noticed Chinese masseuses will be more casual with their clients than American-based ones. I'm a bit more reserved when it comes to services; if I'm paying for something, I'd like to just zone out and not have to use my brain to have a conversation. However, sometimes I've had unexpectedly wonderful discussions from the chatty types, so I try to keep an open mind these days. 

Amy immediately grabbed my attention when she talked about her other business - a construction gig. She told me how she lived in Chinatown, Berwyn, Cicero, Austin and now Oak Park. She'd been in the Chicago area for decades while remodeling homes for other people's projects, house-flipping her own projects and being a landlord. This is exactly the kind of information I wanted to know more about!

Amy regaled how Chinatown used to be full of more Cantonese people, but now it's heavily Mandarin-speaking. She told me how houses were extremely cheap back then and was surprised to hear me say that housing is actually a lot more expensive in Denver in comparison. I think too many transplants came in following Colorado's weed legalization. Things haven't been the same since.

"Ohhh really? I thought a place like that would be very cheap. Okay deep breath..". I relaxed and exhaled as she pressed firmly down the center of my upper back a few times. I heard satisfying cracking noises. 

"20 years ago, Chicago much colder! The area not so nice as now. You guys lucky!"

I laughed. It's true that this year's cold season has been a cakewalk. The previous years weren't anything crazy either. The trick or treaters didn't even need to wear coats. As depressing as climate change is, for selfish reasons I hope things stay this way here. I love seeing flowers and greenery still going strong outside.

Amy told me how she bought a house in Berwyn to flip and sold it last year. She said it's tough right now because tariffs have made materials extremely expensive, "much worse" than the shortages caused by COVID-19. She did much of the remodel work with her small construction company. I asked her if she hired any Mexican immigrants for additional labor, to which she said yes.

"You used to hire them $100 a day but now they want $180", she sighed. "We sold for $595k, but didn't make much money. Spent too much on the work." As she lifted my arm, I winced a bit. "That side not good?"

"Nah, I fell down a few weeks ago." On the way to return a library book, I'd momentarily taken one of my hands off my electric scooter while riding, trying to catch my backpack sliding off my shoulder. I lost control and scraped myself up pretty bad. The shoulder I landed on has been slowly mending. Yuchi was very displeased at the time. Another episode of Ash banging himself up! I don't think I'm ever gonna be able to get that motorcycle.

"Sooo are you gonna try house flipping again or..?" I asked.

"Ah yes! That's just how it goes. Sometimes you make lotta money. Sometimes not so much. But overall you make money. You understand? Like that for renting too. Sometimes you get good renters, make money. Sometimes you go to legal fight to evict."

"THE SQUATTERS!" I exclaimed. I've heard stories about psychotic squatters. In blue states, renters tend to have a lot of legal protection. Unfortunately, some assholes exploit well-intended laws, creating emotionally and financially draining legal battles that landlords must endure in order to get their property back.

"Yes they squat," Amy nodded. "Takes months to get them out." 

I was flipped over on my back now, facing upward as she massaged my arms and hands. "Listen, you smart. I tell you something. You young, buy another property. Not make much money that year, that's okay. It's business property, you write off as tax loss." she looked down at me with a mischievous glint in her eye. Though she wore a face mask, I knew she was grinning.

I smiled and thought to myself, "I bet Yuchi already knows all sorts of this stuff." While he would never become a serial squatter, Yuchi certainly has the potential to be one of the worst ones. When it comes to laws, loopholes and opportunities to exploit a system, Yuchi is a force to be reckoned with. We often laugh about how stereotypically Chinese he is when it comes to money and hustling.

After the massage, we went back out in the front room with the rest of the workers and customers. Amy chatted with a colleague in Cantonese as she swiftly worked my mani pedi. A couple of dudes were sitting in the soaking tubs for their pedicures. An older woman at the nail station next to me offered a wordless, gentle smile. Yep, this is my kinda place. 

I'll definitely be back!
